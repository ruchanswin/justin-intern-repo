# What are the main Agile ceremonies, and what is their purpose?
- Daily Stand-ups are short daily meetings (around 15 minutes). Their purpose is to share each member's progress, raise questions and clarify any confusion. This keeps everyone informed about how you're doing.
- Sprint Planning (Scrum) vs. Continuous Prioritization (Kanban): At the start of each sprint, the team holds a sprint planning to determine the goals and tasks of the sprint (around 1-4 weeks) and the objectives are fixed until the next sprint; while in a Continues Prioritization, work prioritises change rapidly, and each task is set based on the working abilities of the team.
- Retrospectives take place at the end of a sprint or in regular intervals to review the process and achievement of the team, highlight what is done efficiently and what can be improved for later sprints. These retrospectives build confidence, trust and collaboration for the team and allow for constructive feedback.
- Backlog Refinement keeps the backlog clean and organized for future work by repriortising and updating work. This helps avoid confusion and complication and support for later planning.

# How do Agile teams collaborate asynchronously and across time zones?
- They could use shared tools like Jira, Google Docs or Github to update work and everyone can view it.
- They can update their process through stand-ups or via messages and notes.
- They can apply Kanban for their work since Kanban allows each member of the team to work on their own schedules and paces.

# How do Agile ceremonies help with communication and alignment?
- Agile ceremonies ensures transparency and collaboration in the work progress through daily stand-ups, structured planning and clear goals via sprint planning or flexibility and dynamic changes via continuous priortization, and helpful reflection and improvements through retrospectives. Even though each member is distant from each other, these ceremonies bond the team and ensure success.
