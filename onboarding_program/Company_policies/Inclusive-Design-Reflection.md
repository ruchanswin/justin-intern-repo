# Who are considered vulnerable populations, and what challenges might they face in digital spaces?

- Vulnerable populations include: neurodivergent individuals (people who have ADHD or autism), the elderly, non-native speakers, people who have little access to technologies.
- In digital spaces, these groups may feel overwhelmed from cluttered interfaces, frustrated with unpredictable layouts or instructions, and excluded due to tone or jargon.

# What ethical considerations are important when designing or working with neurodivergent individuals? (Hint: Avoid overwhelming UX, respect sensory needs, ensure clear communication.)

- Designs should avoid complex navigation, overwhelming information, excessive animations or flashing elements. These features can make users harder to focus.
- Don’t use long paragraphs without breaks or visual structure, avoid using wording that can be offensive. These help improve readability and comprehension.
- Don’t make users remember each step with no guidance.
- Don’t set short time limits for actions without an option to modify (add more time, pause or save progress).

# How can you make interactions and content more accessible for people with ADHD or Autism? (e.g., simple language, predictable navigation, reducing cognitive load)

- Use clear and consistent layouts.
- Keep navigation simple and predictable so that it is easy for users to find features.
- Break up content into small, manageable chunks.
- Allow users to customise notifications and animations (some visual elements could be distracting, so an option to turn them off is great).
- Offer reminders and prompts for important actions.

# How can we support neurodivergent team members in a professional setting? (Hint: Be clear in communication, respect different working styles, and allow flexibility.)

- Communicate clearly and directly, don't make people guess.
- Respect each member's working style and try to harmonize them to produce greatest results.
- Be flexible in task distribution, deadlines, and schedules.
- Encourage each member to seek help and provide enough help to support their work.

# How can you adjust your communication style to be more inclusive of neurodivergent users and teammates?

- I should be clear and concise about what I want and need.
- I could ask my teammates how they want to be communicated and their working schedules so that I don't interfere their social lives.
- I would demonstrate information in multiple formats (written documents, visual examples or their combination) to help them understand.
- If they are confused about anything, I would provide help to make sure their work meets expectations and deadlines.

# What are some common UX or communication pitfalls that might make Focus Bear less accessible or supportive?

- Cluttered interfaces with too many tasks or pop-ups.
- Confused navigation that could lessen focus.
- From my experience, the Focus Bear mobile app theme is based on the default system theme and the website theme is automatically light theme. It would be better if the theme options are integrated inside the app and website.

# What is one practical change you can make in your work to better support vulnerable populations?

- Be flexible for communication methods (in-person or online meetings) and support documentation in a format that’s easy to read, using bullet points to summarize content concisely, highlighting important content using bold, italic or color backgrounds, and adding pictures or examples where needed. These changes help users process information and pay attention to meetings.

# Practice writing a clear, patient, and supportive response to a hypothetical user struggling with executive functioning

- Hi, I understand it could be difficult for you to manage multiple tasks at once, and it's totally reasonable to feel overwhelming. We are always here to help you. If you don't feel like working on big tasks, you can break them down into simple and small tasks to get you started. It's fine to start slowly, as long as you progress yourself consistently. Remember, even if you only improve by 1 percent every day, you will be 37 times better at the end of the year.
