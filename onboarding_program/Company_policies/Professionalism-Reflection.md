🔍 Research & Learn

# What are examples of professional vs. unprofessional behaviour in a casual work environment? (Hint: Think about communication, meeting etiquette, and teamwork.)

- Profesional behaviours: communicating concisely, being punctual to meetings, standups or any meetups, respecting other people and their ideas, collaborating with your teammates, providing help and constructive feedback
- Unprofesional: Be late to meetings without reasonable purposes, ignoring messages, talk rudely to other people by using offensive wording, lack responsibility for your work, not willing to help your teammates or blame them for all the faults

# What does respectful communication look like in remote teams? (e.g., tone in messages, handling disagreements, responding to feedback)

- You should use a friendly and neutral tone, and should be concise about what you want to express. Using emojis is fine as long as they aren't offensive and vague.
- Respond to other people's messages, even a quick response like "OK" is fine to make sure you actually participate in the conversation.
- When you have questions, feel free to ask but don't be rude and don't forget to thank your team members when you're clear to go.

# How can you give and receive feedback professionally? (e.g., focus on actions, not people; assume good intent; ask for clarification instead of reacting defensively)

- When you give feedback, make sure to focus on the tasks and not the people. Personal attacks do more harm than good.
- When you receive feedback, you can first review the feedback to see if it's valid. You should thank them whether the feedback seems right or wrong to show appreciation. If you're still unclear, you should ask them again to provide explanation and don't overreact.

- Yes, I have. Great teamwork and professionalism are essential for the success and the quality of work. For me, it makes up for roughly 20-30% chance of success. Not all of my past experiences are rainbows and sunshines, some teammates are helpful and some lack responsibility for their work and for other members' efforts. Teamwork and professionalism are effective mainly based on how members interact with each other, how the work is distributed to suit with each member's interest and strength, and how time and efforts are used for tasks. One thing about teamwork and professionalism is that it takes time to grow these behaviours, so constructive feedback is important to help you learn.

We also lacked experience in building a website since our background stemmed from AI and data analytics, and we had conflicts with how the tasks should be distributed, working styles and schedules.
  - As we proceeded the project, we collaborate better since we have adapted to how the project should be done. We also appreciate the help from other classmates and our tutor, who gave us constructive feedback on our work. We also had a united schedule to host meetings for keeping track on the progress of each member, any issues arised and the solutions to them.

It's fine if you lack coordination at the beginning, but you should be serious to adapt and learn to make up for your weaknesses.

# What steps can you take to make sure your communication is clear, respectful, and inclusive?

- You can use external tools like ChatGPT or Grammarly to check your wording. I have used them for a while and they provide great feedback and point out my mistakes carefully (use of wording or grammars, the tones and the audience).
- Provide context for things I talk about, give examples if needed. Don't be too generic and expect other people to guess.
- Give feedback on the tasks, not criticize the person working on them. Use appropriate pronouns and wording to make sure the receivers don't get offended.

# How can you help create a positive, fun, and professional work environment?

- Casual in-person meetings in places like coffee shops or restaurants are great for coordination and connection. These help understand each of the members, and you can discover other aspects of everyone apart from their usual working personality.
- Attend meetings as required, make sure my work meets the standard, don't miss the deadlines and work on well with other colleagues. If any problems arise, it's best practice to solve it internally before reaching out for help. Hiding the problems only makes them more complicated.

# How can you prepare effectively for meetings with supervisors and colleagues?

- Read any documentation provided for the meetings beforehand to make sure you understand the content and ask your supervisors or colleagues on what you're confused about or what you need instructions. Don't ask irrelevant questions or things that are already mentioned in the documentation.
- Engage in the meetings actively, be confident to express your ideas even if it isn't concise or correct. Making mistakes is fine, it helps you learn.
- Take notes on the content of the meetings, you would want to review them later or ask questions after the meetings.

# What would it look like to be proactive during your internship?

- Ask colleagues or supervisors what you can do to contribute to the project, don't just sit still and wait.
- Reach out to other members for help when you're struggled with tasks or need support, you shouldn't expect people to guess how you're doing.
- Tracking your progress using tools like Confluence, Jira or Github Project pages to make sure you don't fall behind and get snowed under work.

# How might you follow up with colleagues or supervisors if you need information/action from them? How would you escalate it further if you don't get a response and when would you escalate?

- If it's not urgent, reach out to them first via mails or text them via other social apps (Facebook, Whatsapp or Discord). If they don't respond at first, you can send them follow-up messages or change your means of communication.
- If it's urgent, call them directly and express what you need from them.
- Be clear and detailed about what you want, give contexts and use appropriate wording or pronouns. Use grammar-checking tools like Grammarly to evaluate your messages.
- Focus on the tasks or information, don't blame someone or personally attack.

# What are some things you won't do?

- Be disrespectful to other colleagues, use offensive wording.
- Skip meetings or any standups without reasonable cause. This shows you're unprofessional and you lack respect to your work.
- Be passive and only express your concerns or ideas when someone asks you.
- Use AI or any other tools blankly without understanding their functionalities. If you don't understand, you can ask AI to explain their way of thinking.
- Share confidential information of the company to other people. For example, if the data is being leaked, it can be used for cyberattacks like phishing or unwanted advertising.
